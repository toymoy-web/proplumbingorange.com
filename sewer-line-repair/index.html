A recurring clog that keeps coming back after snaking is usually a sign the sewer line itself has a structural problem, not just a blockage. We diagnose the actual condition of the line with a camera before recommending a repair method, and offer trenchless options that avoid digging up a driveway or yard wherever the damage allows for it.

Signs

How Orange homeowners describe it

Sewage backing up into the lowest drain.

Usually the tub or a ground-floor toilet, a sign the blockage sits deep in the main line rather than a single fixture.

A soggy or unusually green patch of lawn.

Standing water or lush growth over the sewer line's path often means it's leaking underground.

Sewer odor in the yard or near a cleanout.

A smell that comes and goes near the same spot outside is worth a camera inspection, not just air freshener.

Snaking clears it but it's back within weeks.

A pattern that points to a structural break or heavy root intrusion, not a normal clog.

Process

How we diagnose and repair a sewer line

Camera inspection.

A full run of the line shows cracks, offset joints, root intrusion, or a collapsed section, and exactly where.

Repair method decision.

Trenchless lining or pipe bursting where the line's condition allows it, traditional excavation where it doesn't.

Permit and utility check.

Any dig or trenchless work in the yard or under a driveway gets utility-located and permitted first.

Post-repair camera check.

We re-run the camera after the repair to confirm the line is clear and intact, not just patched.

The Orange picture

Why Orange homes call about this

Old Towne's original clay laterals are aging out.

Homes in Old Towne Orange, some dating to the 1880s, often still run on original clay sewer laterals now 80 to 140 years old. At that age, cracked joints and root intrusion move from an occasional nuisance to a structural repair candidate.

Trenchless lining fits many of these repairs.

Cured-in-place pipe lining can rehabilitate a cracked or root-damaged clay lateral from the inside, without digging up a mature yard or a driveway, though a fully collapsed section still needs traditional excavation done to California Plumbing Code standards.

The homeowner owns the lateral, not just the house side.

In Orange, the property owner is responsible for the sewer lateral from the house to the city main in the street, so a break anywhere along that run, including under a sidewalk, is typically the homeowner's repair, not the city's.

Wet-dry soil cycles stress old joints further.

Clay and sandy loam soil across Orange contracts in the dry season and expands with winter storms, which is a mechanical cause of joint separation on older laterals, separate from and in addition to root intrusion.

FAQ

Questions Orange callers ask

Do you have to dig up my whole yard?

Not always. Trenchless lining repairs many cracked or root-damaged sections without excavation, though a fully collapsed line still needs traditional digging.

How do you know if my line even needs a repair versus just cleaning?

A camera inspection shows the actual condition. If it's a clog with intact pipe, that's drain cleaning. If the pipe itself is cracked or offset, that's a repair.

Who's responsible for the section under the street?

The property owner typically owns the lateral up to the city main connection. We'll tell you clearly if a problem falls outside that boundary.
