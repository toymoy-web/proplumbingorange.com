The neighborhood

the Orange Eichler tracts at a glance

Three tracts.

Fairhaven opened in 1960 in southeast Orange. Fairhills followed in 1962 on the east side. Fairmeadow opened in late 1964 in north Orange and is the largest of the three.

One builder.

Joseph Eichler's company built nearly 350 homes across the three tracts. Preservation groups note they are his only Orange County work.

Historic status.

The City of Orange designated the three tracts as local historic districts in November 2018 and adopted design standards for them. They are not listed on the National Register.

Name recognition.

Owners, preservation groups, and the city all use the tract names, and the three are often discussed together as the Orange Eichlers.

What the age means for plumbing

These homes were built between 1960 and 1964, so any original plumbing that has never been replaced is now more than sixty years old. Many owners have updated parts of their systems over the decades. We cannot know which homes have been updated without looking, and we do not assume.

Eichler homes are generally built on concrete slabs, so some supply and drain lines run beneath the floor. That is general information about the house type, not a claim about your pipes. A pressure test and electronic leak detection can tell you what your own system is doing before anyone cuts concrete.

Worth knowing

Signs worth checking in a slab-foundation home

Feel the floors.

A persistently warm spot on a floor, especially near a bathroom or hallway, is worth a call.

Listen when everything is off.

Running water sounds with no fixture in use can point to a leak in the system.

Track your water bill.

A bill that climbs without a change in habits is often the first clue to a hidden leak.

Collect past records.

Prior repipes or leak repairs show where old and new lines meet, which saves diagnosis time.

Remodeling an Eichler

The 2018 designation came with design standards, so check with the City of Orange before planning changes that affect a home's exterior or defining features. Plumbing permits follow the city's regular process.

Inside, the slab matters for layout. If a fixture sits over concrete, moving it can mean cutting the floor, so settle the kitchen or bath layout before the work starts. Our guide to remodel plumbing in older Orange homes covers the planning questions, and our Eichler plumbing article goes deeper on the tracts.

When a warm floor or a high bill is the clue

A warm spot on a floor, the sound of running water when nothing is on, or a water bill that jumps without explanation can point to a leak under the slab. The first step is confirming it, not guessing where to dig.

If you suspect a leak, shut off the main water valve and watch whether the sounds and the meter movement stop. If they do, the leak is on the house side of the meter. Then call us before anyone opens a floor, because a located leak means a smaller repair.

See our slab leak detection and repair page for how we locate and fix these leaks. If repeated repairs start to pile up, a conversation about repiping may be worth having.

Owner incentives

Preservation groups report that the designation makes owners eligible for the Mills Act property tax program. Ask the city or a tax professional whether it applies to your home before counting on it.

Local FAQ

Questions from Eichler tracts

Which tracts count as Orange's Eichler neighborhoods?

Fairhaven, Fairhills, and Fairmeadow. Together they hold nearly 350 homes and are Eichler's only Orange County work.

Are the Orange Eichler tracts on the National Register?

No. The City of Orange designated them as local historic districts in November 2018. Earlier National Register efforts did not result in a listing.

Does every Eichler home here have the same plumbing?

The three tracts were built over about five years and many owners have renovated since. We look at each house instead of assuming.

Can a leak under the slab be found without breaking concrete?

Electronic leak detection can often locate a leak before any concrete is opened. Call us to talk through what you are noticing.
