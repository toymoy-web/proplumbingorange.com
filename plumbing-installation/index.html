Process

How a plumbing installation project works

Scope and permit.

We confirm what's moving or being added and pull the required permit before any work starts.

Rough-in.

Supply, drain, and vent lines get run to the new fixture locations before walls or floors close up.

Inspection.

Rough-in work gets inspected before it's covered, as required for permitted plumbing work.

Finish and test.

Fixtures get connected and tested for proper flow and drainage once finish work is complete.

The Orange picture

Why Orange homes call about this

Old Towne remodels need extra planning.

Moving a sink or tub in a pre-1950s Old Towne home often means tying into original clay or galvanized lines that weren't designed for a modern layout, which is why we scope these jobs on-site rather than quoting blind.

Eichler slab layouts limit some options.

Fairhaven and Fairhills Eichler homes have supply and drain lines embedded in the slab, which constrains where a new fixture can realistically go without a larger excavation than most remodels budget for.

Permits aren't optional on this kind of work.

Any project that adds or relocates plumbing fixtures requires a permit and inspection under California Plumbing Code standards, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code, regardless of how small the remodel feels.

Newer construction gives more layout freedom.

Santiago Hills and other newer-built areas run PEX supply lines that are far easier to reroute for a remodel than the rigid original lines common in older parts of the city.

Gas line additions have their own code requirements.

Adding a gas range, dryer, or fire feature means sizing the new line correctly against everything already on the system, checked and permitted the same as any other added fixture.

A scope visit catches problems before they become surprises.

Seeing the actual space, existing lines, and access points in person before quoting is why our estimates tend to hold up once work actually starts, rather than growing once walls are opened.

What to do if you smell gas during or after installation.

Leave the area, avoid switching any lights or appliances on or off, and call your gas utility from a safe distance before doing anything else. This applies to any gas work, not just what we install.

FAQ

Questions Orange callers ask

Do I need a permit just to add a bathroom sink?

Generally yes, for any new or relocated fixture. We handle the permit and inspection as part of the job.

Can you work around an existing remodel timeline with other contractors?

Yes, we coordinate rough-in and finish work around cabinetry, tile, and other trades on your schedule.

Is a tub-to-shower conversion a bigger job than it looks?

Sometimes. It often means re-sloping the drain line, which is more involved than swapping the fixture alone.

How long does a typical remodel rough-in take?

A single bathroom usually takes one to two days for rough-in alone, with finish work scheduled once tile and cabinetry are ready.
