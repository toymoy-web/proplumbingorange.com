Signs

How Orange homeowners describe it

Humming but not spinning.

Usually a jam, often clearable without a full replacement.

Leaking from the bottom.

Typically a worn seal, a sign the unit is closer to the end of its life.

No sound at all when switched on.

Sometimes just a tripped reset button, sometimes a dead motor.

Persistent bad odor despite cleaning.

Can mean trapped debris in a spot regular cleaning doesn't reach.

The Orange picture

Why Orange homes call about this

Older Orange homes often have older units.

Original disposals in Old Towne and other established neighborhoods are frequently well past a typical 8 to 12 year service life, which is why replacement is often the more practical call than a repair on an older unit.

Hard water affects these units too.

At 16 to 18 grains of hardness, mineral buildup inside the disposal chamber contributes to jams and reduced grinding performance over time, on top of normal wear.

Newer construction gets straightforward installs.

Santiago Hills and other newer-built homes have standard under-sink layouts that make a disposal swap a quick, same-visit job in most cases.

A reset button solves more calls than people expect.

A tripped reset button on the bottom of the unit is a common, free fix we check before assuming a bigger problem.

Standard installs fit most kitchens without extra work.

Under-sink plumbing in the large majority of Orange homes, regardless of era, follows a standard enough layout that a disposal swap rarely requires anything beyond the unit itself and basic connections.

FAQ

Questions Orange callers ask

Can a jam be fixed without replacing the whole unit?

Often, yes. Clearing a jam or checking the reset button solves many calls without any parts needed.

How long do garbage disposals usually last?

Typically 8 to 12 years with normal use. An older unit that fails is often more practical to replace than repair.

Do you install disposals as part of a bigger remodel?

Yes, we handle disposal installation as a standalone job or as part of a kitchen remodel.

What horsepower disposal do I actually need?

Standard half-horsepower units cover most households. Larger families or heavier kitchen use sometimes benefit from a three-quarter or full-horsepower model, which we can recommend based on your actual use.

Is it normal for a new disposal to sound different from the old one?

Yes, motor and grinding sounds vary by brand and horsepower. If something sounds like grinding metal or a persistent rattle, that's worth a follow-up call.

Do you offer any warranty on a new disposal install?

Yes, both the unit and our installation labor are covered, with the specific terms explained before you approve the job.
