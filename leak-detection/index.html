A jump in the water bill with no visible leak is the single most common way Orange homeowners discover a hidden leak, and it's also the highest-volume plumbing call in this city by a wide margin. We use acoustic and electronic detection to pinpoint the source, whether it's under a slab, inside a wall, or along the yard line, before any digging or demolition is even discussed. If detection points to water under the concrete specifically, that becomes a slab leak repair rather than a surface fix.

Signs

How Orange homeowners describe it

Water bill jumped with no obvious cause.

One of the most common reasons Orange homeowners call, especially when nothing looks visibly wet.

Meter still spins with everything off.

Shut off every fixture and check the meter. If it's still moving, water is going somewhere.

A warm spot on the floor.

Usually a hot water slab leak, common in homes with copper supply lines run through the concrete.

Damp drywall or a musty smell.

Often a wall leak from a supply line or fitting failure inside the wall cavity.

Meter running and you don't know why?

We can usually pinpoint a hidden leak same-day without opening walls or floors.

How we find it

Detection method, not guesswork

Meter and pressure test.

Confirms there's an active leak and roughly how much water is being lost.

Acoustic and electronic tracing.

Listening equipment pinpoints the leak's location along the line, not just the general area.

Slab and wall confirmation.

For suspected slab leaks, thermal and acoustic methods locate the exact spot before any concrete is touched.

Report before repair.

You see where the leak actually is before approving any repair or access work.

The Orange picture

Why Orange homes call about this

Slab-on-grade construction is common here.

Post-1950s Orange neighborhoods are largely built slab-on-grade, meaning supply lines often run through or under the concrete foundation rather than in a crawlspace, which makes precise detection worth far more than guessing before opening concrete.

Eichler copper-in-slab lines.

Fairhaven, Fairhills, and Fairmeadow Eichler tract homes have copper supply lines embedded directly in the slab, a mid-century construction detail that makes those homes especially good candidates for acoustic detection before any repair.

Very hard water accelerates pinhole leaks.

At 16 to 18 grains of hardness, copper pipe here is more prone to pinhole leaks from mineral buildup than in softer-water regions, which shows up as small, hard-to-spot leaks rather than one obvious failure.

A high water bill is often the first real clue.

Leak detection is one of the highest-volume plumbing calls in this city, and a large share of those calls start with someone noticing the bill before noticing any water at all.

Repairs still follow code.

Once a leak is pinpointed, the fix has to meet California Plumbing Code standards, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code, for the pipe material and fitting used, not just stop the immediate drip.

Not sure what's wrong.

"I don't know where it's coming from," what leak detection actually does

If your water bill went up and you can't find a wet spot anywhere, or the meter keeps moving with every fixture off, that's exactly what leak detection is for. It's a diagnostic service that finds the source before any repair is quoted, so you're not paying to open a wall or slab in the wrong place.

FAQ

Questions Orange callers ask

How do you find a leak without digging first?

Acoustic and electronic equipment traces the sound and pattern of moving water through pipe and slab, which pinpoints the source before any digging or demolition.

My water bill is high but I don't see any water. Could it still be a leak?

Yes. Slab leaks especially can run for weeks with no visible sign before water finally surfaces.

Do you repair the leak the same day it's found?

Often, yes, for straightforward access. Slab leaks sometimes need a separate scheduled repair once we know the exact location.
