Signs

How Orange homeowners describe it

A warm spot on the floor.

Usually points to a hot water line leaking under the slab.

Water coming up under the floorboards.

A sign the leak has been active long enough to saturate the sub-floor area.

Unexplained drop in water pressure.

Water escaping under the slab reduces pressure at your fixtures.

Sound of running water with everything off.

Sometimes audible directly through the floor near the leak's location.

The Orange picture

Why Orange homes call about this

Slab-on-grade is the norm here.

Southern California's post-1950s construction boom made slab-on-grade foundations standard across most of Orange, which is exactly why slab leaks are a real, ongoing service category here rather than a rare event.

Eichler homes are a specific risk profile.

Fairhaven, Fairhills, and Fairmeadow Eichler tract homes run copper supply lines directly through the concrete slab, a mid-century design choice that makes these specific neighborhoods more prone to slab leaks as that original copper ages past 60 years.

Hard water accelerates pipe wear.

At 16 to 18 grains of hardness, copper lines under the slab scale and pit faster here than in soft-water regions, which shortens the realistic service life of original slab plumbing.

Insurance often asks for documentation.

Many homeowner policies distinguish between the plumbing repair itself and any resulting water damage. We provide a detection report you can hand to your insurer, without us making any promises about what your specific policy covers.

Repairs follow code, not shortcuts.

Any access repair on a slab or wall leak still has to meet California Plumbing Code standards for the fitting and pipe type used, which is part of why we don't recommend patch jobs that skip inspection on anything beyond a minor fix.

Not sure what's wrong.

"There's a warm spot on my floor," is that a slab leak

A warm patch on tile or carpet, especially near a bathroom or hallway, is one of the clearest early signs of a hot water slab leak. Catching it at this stage, before water surfaces, usually means a smaller, cheaper repair.

FAQ

Questions Orange callers ask

Do you have to break up my whole floor?

No. Acoustic and thermal detection narrows the leak to a small, confirmed area, so we open only that section.

Is my Eichler home more likely to have this problem?

Yes, the original copper embedded in the slab in Fairhaven, Fairhills, and Fairmeadow is now well past 60 years old in many homes, which is the single biggest slab-leak risk factor in this city.

Will insurance cover a slab leak repair?

Coverage depends on your specific policy. We can provide a detection report to support a claim, but we don't make coverage promises.
