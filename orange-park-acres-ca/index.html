The neighborhood

Orange Park Acres at a glance

The community.

Orange Park Acres is a rural-style community of about 1.4 square miles. It is known for large lots, miles of trails, and few streetlights or sidewalks.

The history.

The Orange Park Acres Corporation formed in 1928 to develop 640 acres. The community's property owners organized in 1953 and its present association formed in 1960.

The boundaries.

Local sources describe it as bounded loosely by the hills to the west, Santiago Creek to the north, Irvine Park and the cemetery to the east, and the hills across Chapman Avenue to the south.

The lots.

Most homes here are described as sitting on lots of about 20,000 square feet or larger, and some of the 1970s developments were built as equestrian properties.

A bigger property means different plumbing questions

On a large lot, the distance between the water meter, the house, and any outbuildings can be long. A leak along a long supply line may not show up as a wet spot at the house, which makes a rising water bill the first clue.

A simple test helps. Turn off every fixture and appliance, then watch your water meter. If the dial keeps moving, water is going somewhere. Our leak detection page explains how we find it, and our article on why a water bill keeps climbing covers the other common causes.

Septic or sewer? Find out first

Large rural-style properties can have different wastewater arrangements, and we do not assume either one. Check your records or ask the responsible agency whether your property connects to a municipal sewer or uses a private system. The answer shapes what a drain problem means and who is responsible for the line.

Once you know, our sewer line repair and drain cleaning pages explain how we diagnose a blockage and what the options are.

A mix of city and county

Orange Park Acres has a mix of City and County jurisdictions. Developments from the 1970s were annexed into the City of Orange, and residents have long valued the more rural county designation elsewhere. Plumbing work in California follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), with permits issued by the city or county that has jurisdiction. Because that depends on which side your address falls on, confirm before larger work.

Guest houses, workshops, and stables

Many large properties have more than one building. If a guest house, workshop, or stable has its own water line, tell us when you call. Each structure can have its own shutoff, its own water heater, and its own repair history, and we plan around all of it.

Because every property here is different, we ask a few questions up front. How many buildings have plumbing, how far does the house sit from the street, and where do the main shutoff and meter sit? Short answers to those save a lot of time on the first visit.

Local FAQ

Questions from Orange Park Acres

Is Orange Park Acres in the City of Orange?

It is a mix. Developments from the 1970s were annexed into the City of Orange, while other parts remain under county jurisdiction. Confirm which applies to your address.

Does my Orange Park Acres property use septic or sewer?

It varies by property, and we do not assume. Check your records or ask the responsible agency before planning drain or sewer work.

How do I check for a hidden leak on a large property?

Shut off every fixture and watch your water meter. If it keeps moving, water is escaping somewhere. Call us and we will help locate it.

Do you work on properties with guest houses or stables?

Yes. Tell us about every building with plumbing when you call so we can plan the visit around all of it.
