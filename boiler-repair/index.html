Boilers and radiant heating systems are uncommon in Southern California's mild climate, which means many plumbers simply don't work on them. We do, for the older custom homes and a handful of pre-war Old Towne properties that still run radiant heat instead of forced air.

Signs

How Orange homeowners describe it

Uneven heat or cold spots in radiant floors.

Often a circulation or zone valve issue rather than a full boiler failure.

A boiler that won't ignite or stay lit.

Similar failure points to a water heater's pilot or ignition system, but a different repair process.

Unusual noise from the boiler or pipes.

Kettling or banging often points to mineral buildup or trapped air in the system.

Rising utility bills with no change in use.

A sign the system is running less efficiently than it should, often from scale or a failing component.

Process

How we diagnose a boiler problem

System check.

Circulation pumps, zone valves, and the boiler's ignition and pressure systems are checked in sequence.

Identify the actual fault.

Radiant heat problems are often a valve or circulation issue rather than the boiler unit itself.

Repair or component replacement.

Most repairs involve a specific failed component rather than a full system replacement.

Test across all zones.

We verify even heat across every zone before calling the job done, not just the zone that was complained about.

The Orange picture

Why Orange homes call about this

A genuinely rare system in this climate.

Southern California's mild winters mean most homes were built with forced-air heating, so boiler and radiant systems in Orange are mostly limited to a handful of older custom homes and a few pre-war Old Towne properties that kept their original systems.

Rarity means fewer plumbers will touch it.

Because boiler work is uncommon here, many general plumbers refer this work out rather than service it directly. We keep the training and parts knowledge current specifically because the demand, while small, is real.

Age matters more than usual on these systems.

A radiant system installed decades ago often has original components that are harder to source, which is why diagnosis sometimes takes a bit longer than a comparable water heater repair.

Hard water still applies to these systems.

Orange's 16 to 18 grains of water hardness scales boiler heat exchangers and circulation components the same way it scales water heaters, which is a real contributor to the noise and efficiency complaints we see on older systems.

FAQ

Questions Orange callers ask

Do most plumbers work on boilers in this area?

Not consistently. Radiant heat is uncommon enough here that many plumbers refer this work out. We service it directly.

Is my radiant system worth repairing or should I switch to forced air?

Usually worth repairing if the boiler itself is sound. A full system conversion is a much bigger project we'd only recommend if the boiler has actually failed.

Why is my system heating unevenly across rooms?

Most often a zone valve or circulation issue rather than the boiler itself, which is good news since it's usually a smaller repair.

How do I know if it's time to replace the boiler instead of repairing it?

Age, repair frequency, and efficiency all factor in. We'll walk through the honest tradeoffs rather than push a replacement before it's actually needed.

Can you source parts for an older or discontinued boiler?

In most cases, yes, though it can take longer than a same-visit repair. We'll tell you upfront if a part needs to be special-ordered.
