A running, wobbling, or leaking toilet is usually a same-day fix, and a full replacement is a straightforward same-visit install in most homes. We carry common wax rings, flappers, and fill valves on the truck.

Signs

How Orange homeowners describe it

Running long after each flush.

Typically a flapper or fill valve, one of the most common and least expensive fixes.

Rocking or wobbling when you sit down.

Usually a worn wax ring or a loose base, worth fixing before it leaks onto the floor.

Water pooling at the base.

A sign the wax ring has already failed and water is escaping under the toilet.

Frequent clogging even with normal use.

Can mean a partial blockage in the trap or drain line, not just the toilet itself.

Process

How we handle toilet repairs and installs

Diagnose the actual issue.

Running, wobbling, and clogging each point to a different fix, checked before any parts are replaced.

Repair with stocked parts.

Flappers, fill valves, and wax rings are carried on the truck for same-visit fixes.

Replace when it makes more sense.

An older toilet with multiple issues is sometimes more cost-effective to replace than keep repairing.

Test and confirm the seal.

We test the fill, flush, and base seal before calling the job done.

The Orange picture

Why Orange homes call about this

Older homes often have older toilets.

Original toilets in Old Towne and other established Orange neighborhoods are sometimes decades old, well past the point where a wax ring or internal components are due for replacement.

Water-efficient models are a common upgrade.

Replacing an older toilet with a newer low-flow model is a common request during a bathroom refresh, and we can walk through the tradeoffs honestly rather than push the most expensive option.

Hard water affects internal components.

At 16 to 18 grains of hardness, fill valves and flappers scale and wear faster here, which is part of why a running toilet is one of the more common general repair calls in this city.

Slab foundations affect wax ring failures.

On slab-on-grade homes, a failed wax ring can let water seep toward the slab edge rather than pooling visibly, worth a careful check rather than assuming a wobble is purely cosmetic.

Comfort-height models are a common request during replacement.

Many Orange homeowners replacing an older, lower toilet ask about a taller comfort-height model at the same time, a straightforward upgrade during a standard replacement visit.

FAQ

Questions Orange callers ask

Is a running toilet worth fixing right away?

Yes. Beyond the water waste, a running toilet is one of the cheapest plumbing fixes there is, and it's a quick same-visit repair.

Should I repair or replace an older toilet?

Depends on the toilet's age and condition. A single issue is usually worth repairing. Multiple recurring problems often make replacement the better value.

Do you install water-efficient toilets?

Yes, and we can talk through the real tradeoffs between standard and low-flow models for your household.

How long does a toilet installation usually take?

Most single-toilet replacements complete in under an hour once the new unit is on-site, assuming no unexpected flooring or subfloor issues turn up.

Do you haul away the old toilet?

Yes, removal and disposal of the old unit is included with any replacement.

Can you match a replacement toilet to my existing bathroom style?

We'll walk through options that fit your space and rough-in measurements, not just whatever model happens to be on the truck.
