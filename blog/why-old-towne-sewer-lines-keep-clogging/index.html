Why Do Old Towne Sewer Lines Keep Clogging?

If snaking your drain works for a few weeks and then the clog comes right back, the real cause is probably underground, not in the pipe you can see.

Written by the Orange Pro Plumbing team. Published September 2026.

Root intrusion.Clay laterals.Old Towne tree canopy.

The pattern that shows up again and again.

A specific complaint comes up repeatedly in Old Towne Orange: a drain gets snaked, works fine for a few weeks or months, then backs up again in the exact same way. That pattern almost always points to something structural rather than a normal, one-time clog.

The most common cause in this specific neighborhood is root intrusion into an original clay sewer lateral, some of which date back 80 to 140 years and were never designed with modern root-resistant joints.

Why Old Towne specifically has this problem.

Old Towne's mature tree canopy is one of the neighborhood's defining features and a genuine part of its charm. Tree roots are drawn to the moisture and nutrients inside a sewer line, and they find their way in through the smallest gap at an aging clay joint.

Once roots get a foothold inside the pipe, they grow into a dense mass that catches paper and debris, which is what actually causes the backup. Snaking cuts a channel through that mass temporarily, but it doesn't remove the roots or repair the joint that let them in, which is why the clog returns.

What actually breaks the cycle.

A camera inspection is the only way to confirm root intrusion versus a simple blockage, and it shows exactly where along the line the problem sits. From there, hydro jetting clears the root mass far more thoroughly than cabling alone, since it scours the full interior of the pipe rather than just punching through the center.

For a line with significant structural damage from years of root pressure, sewer line repair, including trenchless lining options that don't require excavating a mature yard, is often the longer-term fix once jetting alone stops holding.

What homeowners in Old Towne should actually do.

If a drain has needed snaking more than once in a year, especially the same drain, it's worth a camera inspection rather than repeating the same temporary fix. The cost of confirming the actual cause is small compared to a full sewage backup from a line that finally gives out.

This is a genuinely common issue across this specific part of the city, not a sign that something was done wrong with the house. It's simply a consequence of century-old materials meeting the neighborhood's beautiful, mature trees, and it's manageable once you know what's actually happening underground.

What hydro jetting actually does differently than snaking.

A cable snake punches a channel through a blockage, clearing enough space for water to flow again. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the entire interior diameter of the pipe, removing root mass, grease buildup, and debris along the full length of the run, not just a narrow path through the center.

For a line with established root intrusion, that thoroughness is the difference between a fix that holds for years and one that needs repeating every few months. Jetting isn't always the first thing to try, but it's usually the right escalation once snaking alone stops working.

Prevention between now and your next inspection.

Avoiding heavy root-attracting plantings directly over a known sewer line path can slow future intrusion, though it won't stop it entirely if the line already has open joints. A periodic camera check, every few years rather than only after a backup, catches developing root growth before it becomes a full blockage.

For homeowners who've already had one root-related repair, this kind of preventive check is usually worth the modest cost compared to another emergency call.

Talking to neighbors can help too.

Old Towne's lots and original infrastructure often follow similar patterns block by block. If a neighbor has already dealt with root intrusion or a lateral repair, that's useful, informal information about what your own line might be facing, especially on a street with a similar tree canopy and housing age.

Common questions.

Is it worth having a plumber on retainer for an older home?

Not typically necessary, though building a relationship with one you trust makes future calls, routine or urgent, more straightforward.

Do newer sewer liner materials last as long as original clay?

Modern trenchless lining materials are generally rated for multiple decades of service, comparable to or exceeding traditional pipe materials.

Does a repaired section need the same maintenance as the rest of the line?

Once repaired, that section is typically more resistant to root intrusion than the surrounding older pipe, requiring less frequent attention.

Will insurance help cover sewer line repair from root damage?

Coverage varies significantly by policy, and root-related damage is often treated as a maintenance issue rather than a covered peril, so checking your specific policy matters.

Does root intrusion ever go away on its own?

No, roots don't retreat once established. Left alone, the intrusion typically worsens over time rather than resolving.

Is there a way to know how bad root intrusion is without a camera?

Not reliably. Surface symptoms like slow drains don't indicate severity, which is exactly why a camera inspection is the standard diagnostic step.

Is hydro jetting safe for older clay pipe?

Yes, when performed by a trained technician who adjusts pressure appropriately for the pipe's condition and material, which is why this isn't a DIY-rentable service.

Will chemical root killers solve this permanently?

They can slow regrowth temporarily but don't repair the underlying joint damage that let roots in. A camera inspection and, if needed, a structural repair address the actual cause.

How do I know if it's roots versus just grease or paper buildup?

A camera inspection distinguishes the two clearly. Root intrusion has a distinct visual signature that's different from a grease clog or a foreign object.
