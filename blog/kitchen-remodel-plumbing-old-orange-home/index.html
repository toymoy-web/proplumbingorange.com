What an Older Orange Kitchen Remodel Means for Plumbing

Moving a sink or adding an island with plumbing sounds simple until you're working around 70-year-old pipe. Here's what actually changes by housing era.

Written by the Orange Pro Plumbing team. Published September 2026.

Old Towne.Eichler slab.Newer construction.

Why the same remodel plan costs different amounts in different Orange homes.

A kitchen remodel that relocates a sink or adds an island with its own plumbing is a completely different job depending on the home's construction era. The finishes might look identical in the final result, but what's happening under the floor varies enormously.

Old Towne homes: working around original lines.

In a pre-1950s Old Towne home, existing supply and drain lines are often original galvanized or early copper, sized and routed for a much simpler kitchen layout than most modern remodels call for. Relocating a sink usually means tying into these older lines in a way that requires more careful planning than a newer home would.

This is also a natural point to catch problems early. If a plumbing installation project opens up a wall or floor and finds corroded galvanized pipe nearby, it's often more cost-effective to address it during the remodel than to patch around it and deal with a separate failure later.

Eichler homes: real layout constraints.

Eichler-tract kitchens present a different challenge entirely. With supply and drain lines embedded in the slab, moving a sink or adding a wet island isn't just a matter of running new pipe through a wall. It may require breaking into the slab itself, which is a bigger scope than most homeowners initially plan for.

This doesn't mean a remodel isn't possible, but it does mean the plumbing scope should be assessed on-site early in the planning process, before cabinetry and layout decisions are finalized around an assumption that might not hold.

Newer construction: more flexibility, same permit rules.

Homes built from the 1990s onward, including much of Santiago Hills, typically run PEX supply lines that are far easier to reroute for a remodel than older rigid materials. That gives more real layout flexibility.

Regardless of era, any remodel that adds or relocates fixtures requires a permit and inspection under California Plumbing Code standards. That's true whether the job is straightforward or complicated, and it's not something worth skipping to save time.

The practical takeaway.

Get the plumbing scope assessed on-site before finalizing a kitchen layout, especially in an older or Eichler-era home where assumptions from a generic remodel plan may not hold. It's a much easier conversation before cabinets are ordered than after.

How to sequence plumbing work with other trades.

Plumbing rough-in needs to happen before drywall, flooring, or cabinetry go in, which means it has to be scheduled early in a remodel's timeline, not treated as an afterthought once the design is finalized. Getting this sequence wrong is one of the most common causes of remodel delays.

A good general contractor will coordinate this automatically, but if you're managing separate trades yourself, confirm the plumbing scope and rough-in schedule before ordering cabinets or scheduling tile work, since a layout change discovered late can push the whole project back.

Budgeting realistically for the plumbing portion.

Plumbing typically represents a meaningful share of a kitchen remodel's total cost, more in an older or Eichler-era home than in newer construction. Getting a plumbing-specific scope and quote early, before finalizing a broader remodel budget, avoids the common problem of discovering the true cost only after cabinets are already ordered.

Questions worth asking your contractor upfront.

Before finalizing a remodel plan, ask directly whether the plumbing scope has been assessed on-site, whether a permit will be pulled for any relocated fixtures, and what happens if unexpected pipe condition is discovered once work begins.

A contractor who can answer these clearly and specifically, rather than in vague generalities, is a good sign you're working with someone who has actually planned the plumbing portion rather than treating it as an afterthought to the visible finishes.

Common questions.

Is plumbing scope review free?

Most plumbers include an on-site scope visit at no charge before providing a written quote.

Does adding a bathroom always require a full permit process?

Yes, adding any new fixture location triggers permit and inspection requirements under California code.

Does a permit inspection ever delay a remodel significantly?

Occasionally, if scheduling with the inspector's office takes time, which is another reason to pull permits early rather than last minute.

Can I live in the house during plumbing rough-in work?

Usually yes, though water may be off intermittently in affected areas during active work.

Can I keep my existing fixtures during a plumbing rough-in?

Sometimes, if the layout isn't changing significantly, though a full remodel often involves fixture replacement as part of the overall plan.

Does moving a dishwasher require the same plumbing consideration as a sink?

Yes, dishwasher relocation involves both supply and drain connections, similar in scope to relocating a sink.

How far in advance should I schedule remodel plumbing work?

As early as possible in the planning process, ideally before cabinetry and layout are fully finalized.

Can plumbing rough-in work happen while other renovation work continues?

Often yes, with proper sequencing and coordination, though certain stages need to complete before drywall or flooring proceeds.

Does a remodel plumbing permit expire if the project takes a while?

Permits typically have a validity window with required inspections along the way, worth tracking especially on a longer project.

Can plumbing rough-in be redone if plans change mid-remodel?

Sometimes, but it adds real cost and delay. Locking in the layout before rough-in starts avoids this entirely.

Can I add a kitchen island with a sink to an Eichler home?

Sometimes, but it usually requires breaking into the slab to run new drain and supply lines, which is a bigger scope than a standard island install. Worth assessing on-site before committing to the layout.
