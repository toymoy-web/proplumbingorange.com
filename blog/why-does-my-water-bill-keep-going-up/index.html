Why Does My Water Bill Keep Going Up? A Guide

This is the single most common way Orange homeowners discover a plumbing problem: not a wet spot, but a number on a bill that doesn't add up.

Written by the Orange Pro Plumbing team. Published September 2026.

Meter check.Common hidden causes.When to call.

Why this is worth taking seriously.

A water bill that's crept up over a few months, or jumped suddenly with no change in household habits, is one of the most common reasons Orange homeowners end up calling about a plumbing issue. It's also one of the easiest signs to dismiss, since nothing looks visibly wrong.

The simple check you can do yourself first.

Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water, then check your water meter. If the meter is still moving with everything off, water is going somewhere on your property, even if you can't see it yet. This single test is the fastest way to confirm whether a hidden leak is actually the cause before calling anyone.

What actually turns out to be the cause, most often.

A running toilet that's silent enough not to be noticed is one of the most common causes of a higher-than-expected bill, and one of the cheapest to fix once identified. A worn flapper or fill valve can waste a surprising amount of water over weeks without ever making an obvious sound.

A slab leak is the more serious possibility, especially in Eichler-era homes or any post-1950s construction where supply lines run through the slab. These leaks often run for weeks before any visible sign appears, which is exactly why a rising bill is frequently the first real clue.

Irrigation system leaks, a leaking water heater, and worn fixture valves round out the most common causes we actually find once someone calls.

Why guessing costs more than checking.

Without confirming the actual source, it's easy to assume the wrong cause, replace a fixture that wasn't the problem, and still have the same high bill next month. Leak detection pinpoints the actual source using acoustic and electronic equipment before any repair is recommended, which avoids paying to fix the wrong thing.

If detection points to a leak under the slab specifically, that's a distinct next step, slab leak repair, with the access point already narrowed down rather than a guess.

The bottom line.

A rising water bill is data, not something to shrug off. Checking the meter yourself takes five minutes and either rules out a leak entirely or confirms it's worth a professional look, which is a much better position to be in than waiting for water to actually show up somewhere in the house.

What a leak detection visit actually involves.

A technician starts by confirming the leak with a meter and pressure test, then uses acoustic and electronic tracing equipment to narrow down the location along your line. This typically takes under an hour for a straightforward case.

You'll get a clear answer on where the water is going before any repair is recommended, which means the next step, whether it's a simple fixture fix or a slab leak repair, is based on confirmed information rather than a guess.

What happens after the leak is found.

Once detection confirms the location, the repair itself is scoped and quoted separately, whether that's a simple fixture fix, a supply line repair, or a slab leak repair, each done to California Plumbing Code standards. You'll know the real cost before approving anything, which is a very different experience than guessing your way through fixture replacements one at a time.

Most homeowners see their bill return to normal within one billing cycle after the actual leak is repaired, which is itself useful confirmation that the right problem was found and fixed.

A quick reference before you call.

Note your meter reading with everything off, roughly how much your bill has increased and over what period, and whether you've noticed any physical signs like a warm spot, damp area, or sound of running water. Having these details ready speeds up the diagnostic process once a technician arrives.

Common questions.

Is checking for leaks worth doing even without a rising bill?

A periodic meter check as routine maintenance can catch a slow leak before it meaningfully affects your bill at all.

Could a high bill be a meter reading error rather than a leak?

Occasionally, though it's less common than an actual leak. Your water utility can review recent readings if you suspect a billing error.

Does a pool or spa affect water bill troubleshooting?

Yes, pool equipment and auto-fill systems are worth checking separately, since a pool leak can also drive up a water bill significantly.

How soon after a repair should I expect to see the bill normalize?

Typically within one full billing cycle, assuming the confirmed leak was the sole cause of the increase.

Could a high bill be from irrigation rather than household plumbing?

Yes, a stuck irrigation valve or a cracked line is a common cause worth checking separately from indoor fixtures.

Will the water utility itself investigate a high bill?

Some utilities offer a basic leak check or bill review, though they generally won't diagnose or locate a leak on your private plumbing the way a plumber would.

Can a leak be intermittent and still show up on the meter test?

Yes, some leaks are pressure or temperature dependent and may not show constantly, which is why a professional visit with more sensitive equipment sometimes catches what a basic meter check misses.

How much can a hidden leak actually add to a water bill?

It varies widely depending on the leak's size and how long it's been running, but even a small, continuous leak adds up meaningfully over a full billing cycle.

Is checking the meter myself reliable?

Yes, it's a straightforward, accurate first test. If the meter moves with everything off, that confirms an active leak somewhere on your line.
