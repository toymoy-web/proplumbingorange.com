Santiago Hills: What's Different About the Plumbing

Newer construction doesn't mean plumbing problems disappear, it just means different ones show up, and at a different pace than older parts of the city.

Written by the Orange Pro Plumbing team. Published September 2026.

1990s construction.PEX supply lines.A different risk profile.

A different era, a different set of issues.

Santiago Hills, developed mostly in the 1990s as a planned community, gives Orange a genuinely different housing profile than the pre-1950s core or the mid-century Eichler tracts. That newer construction generally means PEX and modern copper supply lines rather than the galvanized or slab-embedded materials common elsewhere in the city.

What newer construction actually solves.

PEX is more flexible than rigid copper or galvanized pipe, which makes it more tolerant of the seasonal ground movement from this region's clay and sandy loam soil. It also doesn't corrode internally the way galvanized steel does, which removes one of the most common failure modes seen in Old Towne homes.

This generally means fewer whole-system repiping calls in Santiago Hills homes compared to the city's older neighborhoods, since the underlying material is less prone to the age-related failures that drive most repiping decisions.

What newer construction doesn't solve.

Slab-on-grade foundations are just as standard in Santiago Hills as anywhere else built after 1950 in this region, which means slab leak risk doesn't disappear just because the pipe material is newer. A supply line can still develop a leak from a manufacturing defect, an installation error, or simple age, even in PEX or modern copper.

Water heaters in these homes are subject to the same 16 to 18 grains of hardness as the rest of the city, which means scaling and the maintenance that comes with it apply here just as much as in an older Old Towne home.

What this means for a Santiago Hills homeowner.

Expect fewer surprises from the supply and drain material itself, but don't assume newer construction means no plumbing maintenance is needed. Water heater descaling, fixture wear, and the occasional slab leak are all still realistic over a home's life here, just without the added layer of century-old original materials complicating the picture.

What to expect from a service call in this neighborhood.

A typical Santiago Hills service call looks less like a repair and more like routine maintenance, water heater descaling, a fixture replacement, or a leak detection check given the home's age. Technicians familiar with this tract's construction era know to expect PEX supply lines and standard modern layouts, which usually means a faster diagnosis than in an older or architecturally unusual home.

That said, don't assume newer means immune. A slab leak or a water heater failure can happen in a 1990s home just as it can anywhere else, and it's worth the same detection-first approach rather than assuming age alone rules it out.

How this compares across the rest of the network's coverage area.

Santiago Hills' plumbing profile has more in common with newer construction in Irvine's master-planned villages or Brea's Olinda Ranch than with Orange's own Old Towne or Eichler tracts, despite being in the same city. Housing era, more than city boundary, is usually the better predictor of what a home's plumbing actually needs.

A quick reference for what's typical here.

Most Santiago Hills plumbing calls fall into routine categories: water heater replacement as original units reach the end of their service life, occasional fixture repairs, and periodic leak detection checks consistent with the rest of the city's water hardness. Major repiping projects are the exception here, not the norm, given the newer materials used throughout the tract.

Common questions.

Would a Santiago Hills remodel need the same permits as elsewhere?

Yes, permit requirements apply the same regardless of the home's age or construction era.

Is Santiago Hills served by the same emergency dispatch as the rest of Orange?

Yes, the same 24/7 line covers this neighborhood with no separate wait time or process.

Would a Santiago Hills home ever need slab leak detection?

It's less common given the newer construction, but not impossible, and the same detection-first approach still applies if a leak is suspected.

Are there any older pockets of housing within Santiago Hills itself?

The tract is fairly uniform in construction era, without the older pockets you'd find layered into a longer-established neighborhood.

Would a newer water heater in Santiago Hills still benefit from a filter?

Yes, given the countywide hardness, a filter or softener can extend any water heater's service life regardless of the home's age.

Do Santiago Hills homes ever have any original 1990s plumbing issues?

Occasionally, mostly related to original fixture wear rather than pipe material failure, consistent with the age of the housing stock.

Is there a specific water heater brand common to this tract?

Builder-grade units from that era vary, and by now most original units have already been replaced at least once given typical lifespans.

Would a newer Santiago Hills home ever need repiping?

It's uncommon but not impossible, usually only if there's a specific installation defect rather than general age-related wear.

Are there any Santiago Hills-specific water quality concerns beyond hardness?

No documented issues beyond the same countywide hardness profile shared with the rest of Orange.

Do HOA rules in Santiago Hills affect plumbing work?

Some communities have exterior modification rules that could apply to visible work like new venting, worth checking before a larger project.

Are Santiago Hills homes under any kind of builder warranty still?

Original builder warranties on homes this age have generally expired, so standard service and maintenance apply the same as any other home past its warranty period.

Are Santiago Hills homes less likely to need a full repipe?

Generally yes, given the newer, more corrosion-resistant materials used in construction, though individual issues like a fixture leak or a slab leak can still occur regardless of overall pipe age.
