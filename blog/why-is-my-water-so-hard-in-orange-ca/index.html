Why Is My Water So Hard in Orange, CA? A Guide

If your water heater scales up faster than the manual says it should, or your fixtures develop white crust within months of cleaning, hardness is almost certainly why.

Written by the Orange Pro Plumbing team. Published September 2026.

16-18 grains hardness.OC Groundwater Basin.Very hard.

How hard is Orange's water, actually?

Orange's municipal supply runs 16 to 18 grains of hardness, which puts it firmly in the "very hard" category on the standard water hardness scale. That number comes from the mineral content of the Orange County Groundwater Basin, the aquifer that supplies most of north and central Orange County, including Orange itself.

For comparison, water is generally considered soft below 3.5 grains and moderately hard up to about 10.5. At 16 to 18 grains, Orange sits well above even the "hard" category, which is part of why scale buildup here isn't a minor cosmetic issue, it's a real factor in how long fixtures and appliances last.

What hard water actually does to your plumbing.

The most visible effect is scale, the white or chalky buildup you see around faucet aerators, showerheads, and glass shower doors. That same mineral buildup happens inside pipes and inside your water heater, just where you can't see it.

Inside a standard tank water heater, sediment settles at the bottom and insulates the heating element or burner from the water above it, which is part of why an older tank starts rumbling or popping before it fails outright. Inside a tankless unit, scale narrows the heat exchanger itself, which is why manufacturers recommend an annual flush in hard water areas like this one, not as an upsell but as basic maintenance.

Does a water softener actually help?

A whole-home water softener addresses the mineral content at the source, which reduces scale buildup in pipes, water heaters, and fixtures over time. It's a legitimate fix, though it's a separate system and installation from a standard plumbing repair.

Short of a full softener, a few things genuinely help: descaling a tankless unit annually, flushing sediment from a tank water heater periodically, and replacing aerators and showerheads when they visibly crust over rather than trying to clean them indefinitely.

What this means if you're shopping for a new water heater.

If you're comparing tank and tankless options for an Orange home, hardness is worth factoring into the decision, not just upfront cost. A tankless unit lasts 20-plus years with regular maintenance, but that maintenance matters more here than it would in a soft-water region.

Homes in Brea deal with a somewhat different mineral profile, since Brea blends imported Metropolitan Water District supply with a private water company's sources rather than drawing purely on the same groundwater basin as Orange. If you're comparing notes with a neighbor in a different city, that's part of why your experience with scale might not match theirs exactly.

What a descaling visit actually involves.

A tankless descale isn't a quick rinse. A technician circulates a descaling solution through the unit's heat exchanger with a pump, typically for 45 minutes to an hour, then flushes the system clean. It's a maintenance visit, not a repair, and it's meant to happen before scale becomes a performance problem.

For a tank water heater, sediment flushing is simpler: draining a portion of the tank periodically to clear settled mineral buildup before it insulates the burner or element. Neither service takes long, and both cost far less than the repair or early replacement that skipping them tends to cause.

How this compares to neighboring cities.

Not every Orange County city draws from the same source. Cities on the Orange County Groundwater Basin, including Orange itself, generally see similarly high hardness readings, while cities blending in imported water from other sources sometimes see a slightly different mineral profile.

This matters mainly if you're moving between cities in the county and trying to figure out why a water heater that lasted 15 years at your old address seems to be scaling faster at the new one. The answer is often simply a difference in the local water source, not anything wrong with the new unit.

The bottom line for Orange homeowners.

Hardness isn't something you fix by ignoring it or something that requires panic either. It's a known, measurable factor in this specific city that changes the maintenance math on water heaters and fixtures. What to do about it: build a simple annual habit, a tankless flush, a tank sediment check, a look at aerators and showerheads, and most of hard water's real costs never show up as an emergency repair.

Common questions.

Is there a way to test water hardness at home?

Yes, inexpensive test strips are available at most hardware stores, though a professional reading is more precise if you're making a decision based on the result.

Does the tankless-versus-tank hardness issue apply to whole-home filtration too?

A whole-home filter or softener addresses hardness at the source and reduces the maintenance burden on every fixture and appliance, not just the water heater, though it's a separate system and installation.

Will a plumber tell me honestly if a softener isn't worth it for my situation?

Yes, it depends on your household's actual usage and budget, and a good technician will walk through the real tradeoffs rather than push an unnecessary add-on.

Is descaling something I can do myself?

Some homeowners attempt it with a DIY kit, but a professional descale ensures the solution reaches the full heat exchanger and is flushed completely, which is harder to guarantee with a basic at-home approach.

Is Orange's water safe to drink even though it's hard?

Yes, water hardness is a mineral content issue, not a safety issue. Hard water meets the same safety standards as soft water, it's just harder on plumbing and appliances over time.

How often should I flush my tankless water heater given this hardness?

Annually is the standard recommendation for this area. Skipping it is one of the most common reasons a tankless unit needs repair before it should.
