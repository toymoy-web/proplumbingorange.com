The neighborhood

Old Towne Orange at a glance

The district.

The Old Towne, Orange Historic District covers about one square mile centered on Plaza Park. It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on July 11, 1997.

The housing.

The City of Orange's historic context statement describes homes here dating from the 1880s onward, with Craftsman bungalow, Mission and Spanish Revival, and Classical Revival styles.

The mix.

Neighborhood housing data shows detached houses alongside small multi-unit buildings, so the plumbing conversation can change from one block to the next.

The landmarks.

Chapman University sits at Glassell and Palm, a few blocks from the Plaza, which anchors the area's shops and restaurants.

Plumbing in a historic district home

Owning a home in Old Towne means living on a street people visit to photograph. It also means your house may have been built long before modern plumbing codes, then updated in pieces over many decades. Some owners here have repiped or replaced their sewer lines. Others have never opened a wall.

We start by finding out which kind of house you have. Homes that are many decades old can have supply pipes and drain lines near the end of their useful life, and the only reliable way to know is to look. A camera inspection of the sewer line gives you facts instead of guesses.

Worth knowing

A practical checklist for older-home owners

Find your main shutoff.

Know where the main water valve is and that it turns. Test it once so you are not learning during a leak.

Watch how drains behave.

Note which drain is slow and how often. A pattern tells a plumber more than a single clog.

Ask for camera footage.

When a sewer line is inspected, ask to see the video and keep a copy with your home records.

Keep repair records.

Save invoices and permits. They show which parts of the house have already been updated.

Before you buy and before you remodel

If you are buying in Old Towne, a plumbing check is worth doing alongside the general inspection. We wrote a plain-language guide on what to check before you close. If you are planning a kitchen or bath project, our piece on remodel plumbing in older Orange homes covers what to settle before the walls open.

Plumbing work in California follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), with permits issued locally. Any historic review that applies to your property is set by the City of Orange, so if a project touches the outside of the house or visible features, ask the city before work starts.

Mature trees and slow drains

Mature trees are part of what makes older neighborhoods pleasant, and tree roots can find their way into sewer lines wherever those lines exist. If the same drain backs up again soon after being cleared, a camera inspection can show whether roots or a damaged section are the cause.

Our article on why Old Towne drains keep clogging walks through the usual causes, and our sewer line repair page explains the options once the cause is confirmed.

The blocks around the Plaza

The city's historic context statement also identifies the Cypress Street Barrio in the northwest part of Old Towne, bounded by Rose Avenue, Glassell Street, Almond Street, and the railroad tracks. Settlement there began in the 1890s, and small bungalows and early commercial buildings sit side by side.

Whatever the block, we take the same approach. We look first, explain what we find in plain terms, and let you decide what happens next.

When you interview any plumber for an older home, ask four practical questions. Will you inspect the lines before quoting, will you pull a permit when one is required, will you explain which parts are original and which have been replaced, and will you put the scope in writing before work begins? Clear answers to those tell you a lot.

Local FAQ

Questions from Old Towne

Is Old Towne a separate city from Orange?

No. Old Towne is the historic center of the City of Orange, and the historic district surrounds Plaza Park.

Do I need a plumber who knows older homes?

Experience with older homes helps, because layouts and materials vary widely. Whatever the house, ask any plumber to explain what they see before work begins.

Will the city need to approve plumbing work on a historic home?

Plumbing permits follow the City of Orange's rules. Historic review, where it applies, concerns a property's exterior and visible features, so ask the city's planning staff about your specific project.

Can the sewer line be inspected before I buy?

A camera inspection of the sewer line is a common step when buying an older home. Call us and we will talk through the timing and what to look for.
