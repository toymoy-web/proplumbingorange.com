The Orange picture

Why plumbing here isn't one-size-fits-all

Every era of housing at once.

Orange runs from 1880s Old Towne Victorians with original clay laterals to 1960s Eichler tracts with copper embedded in the slab to 1990s Santiago Hills homes on modern PEX. What your house needs depends heavily on which one of those it is.

Very hard water.

Orange's municipal supply runs 16 to 18 grains of hardness, which scales water heaters and pits copper pipe faster than in softer-water regions. It's a real factor in how often fixtures and units need service here.

Slab-on-grade construction.

Most post-1950s Orange homes sit on a concrete slab rather than a crawlspace, which is why slab leak detection and repiping are real, ongoing service categories in this city rather than rare events.

Wet-dry soil cycles.

Clay and sandy loam soil across Orange contracts in the dry season and expands with winter storms, straining old pipe joints and sewer connections, especially in Old Towne's older laterals.

Licensed work matters here.

California requires a C-36 license for any plumbing job over $500 in labor and materials. On a city with this much variation in pipe age and material, that license is what stands between a real fix and a patch that fails again in a year.
