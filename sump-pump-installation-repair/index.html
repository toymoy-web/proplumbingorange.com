Process

How we handle sump pump work

Assess the actual water source.

Not every wet crawlspace needs a pump. Sometimes grading or drainage fixes the problem instead.

Size the system.

Pump capacity is matched to the property's actual water intrusion volume, not a generic default.

Install or repair.

New installs include the pit, pump, and discharge line. Repairs target the specific failed component.

Test under real conditions.

We run water through the system to confirm the float switch and pump activate correctly.

The Orange picture

Why Orange homes call about this

Santiago Creek shapes where this matters.

Properties near Santiago Creek and other low-lying parts of Orange have historically been more prone to water intrusion during major storms, which is where a sump pump system earns its cost, unlike most of the city where it's unnecessary.

Most Orange homes genuinely don't need one.

Southern California's dry climate and typical grading mean sump pumps aren't standard here the way they are in wetter regions. We'll say so honestly if a property's issue is better solved with drainage or grading work instead.

Battery backup matters more during real storms.

Power outages are more likely during the exact heavy rain events that cause flooding, which is why we recommend battery backup on any system protecting a finished or valuable space.

Older systems are often undersized.

A sump pump installed years ago for a smaller water intrusion problem sometimes can't keep up if drainage patterns on the property have changed since, worth reassessing rather than assuming the original sizing still fits.

Discharge routing matters as much as the pump itself.

A poorly routed discharge line that dumps water right back toward the foundation defeats the purpose of the system, which is why we check the full path, not just the pump and pit, on every install.

What to do if the pump fails during a storm.

Avoid standing in accumulated water near the pump or its electrical connection. Shut off power to the unit at the breaker if it's sparking or visibly damaged, and call rather than attempt an electrical fix near standing water yourself.

FAQ

Questions Orange callers ask

Does my property actually need a sump pump?

Only if there's a real, recurring water intrusion problem. We assess honestly rather than recommend a system that isn't needed.

What happens if the power goes out during a storm?

Without battery backup, the pump stops working exactly when it's needed most, which is why we recommend backup power for any system protecting a finished space.

Can you fix my existing pump instead of replacing it?

Often, yes. A failed float switch or a stuck component is a common, less expensive repair compared to full replacement.

How often does a sump pump need maintenance?

An annual check of the float switch and a test run is enough for most systems, more often if the pump runs frequently during wet seasons.
