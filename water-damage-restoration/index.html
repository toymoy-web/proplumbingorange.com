Process

How water damage response works

Extraction.

Standing water gets pumped and extracted immediately to stop further absorption into flooring and walls.

Moisture mapping.

Moisture meters identify exactly how far water has traveled, often further than what's visible.

Drying and dehumidification.

Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers dry the affected area to prevent mold growth.

Documentation.

Photos and moisture readings are documented for your insurance claim, without us making promises about what your policy covers.

The Orange picture

Why Orange homes call about this

Slab leaks are a real source of water damage here.

Post-1950s Orange homes are largely slab-on-grade, and a slab leak that runs undetected for even a few days can soak flooring and drywall well beyond the leak's actual location before anyone notices.

Old Towne's original plumbing raises the stakes.

Aging galvanized supply lines and clay laterals in Old Towne homes are more prone to sudden failure than newer materials, which is part of why a burst pipe here can escalate to real water damage faster than in newer construction.

Response time matters more than the source.

Whether the water came from a failed water heater, a burst supply line, or a sewer backup, the drying and extraction process is the same. Starting within hours meaningfully limits how much needs to be replaced versus dried and saved.

This line is separate from standard repair scheduling.

Water damage response runs on its own 24/7 dispatch, separate from standard plumbing repair scheduling, so a flooding emergency doesn't wait behind a routine appointment queue.

Mold risk increases the longer water sits.

Moisture left unaddressed for more than 24 to 48 hours meaningfully raises the risk of mold growth, which is the main reason fast extraction and drying matter more than which specific plumbing failure caused the water.

What to do before help arrives.

If it's safe to reach, shut off the water source at the main or the fixture. Avoid walking through standing water near outlets or a breaker panel, and move valuables and electronics away from the wet area rather than waiting for a crew to arrive.

FAQ

Questions Orange callers ask

How fast can someone respond?

The water damage line runs 24/7, including nights, weekends, and holidays, for active water emergencies.

Will insurance cover this?

Many homeowner policies cover sudden water damage from a burst pipe or similar failure. We document the damage for your claim but can't make promises about your specific policy.

Do you also fix the plumbing problem that caused the damage?

The water damage crew handles extraction and drying. The underlying plumbing repair is typically scheduled alongside it through our standard plumbing service.

How long does drying typically take?

Most rooms dry within three to five days with proper air movement and dehumidification, verified with follow-up moisture readings rather than a guess.
